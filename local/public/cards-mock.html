The upside survives untouched: Groceries moved $420 → $496.20 because the card data arrived, and Food, Transportation and Misc are populated for the first time. F6 — your coloring rule, and the two things it needs that don't exist yet. Debt Payments at $1,793.33 / $2,000.00 = 89.7% renders yellow above. The rule in full: > target → green, > 0.8 × target → yellow, ≤ 0.8 × target → red, = target → gray.

That is statusFor()'s existing fund direction (shared/csp.js:58) with two deviations, and both need a decision:
(a) the thresholds differ. The shared fund band is FUND_GOOD = 0.95 / FUND_WARN = 0.75; yours is 1.0 / 0.8 — stricter at both ends. At 96% of target the shared band says green and your rule says yellow. So this is a per-item threshold pair, not just a per-item direction. Proposal: data.direction: "fund" plus optional data.good_at / data.warn_at on the item, defaulting to the shared constants, so one item can be strict without re-tuning Savings and Investments.
(b) gray is a fourth status. statusFor() returns only good/warn/bad, and csp.html styles exactly those three. "Exactly on target" needs an even status and a .delta.even rule — both are one line each (added to this mockup's <style>). Worth knowing it will almost never fire: these are monthly averages over a 3-month window, so landing on the cent is near-impossible. If you meant "within a dollar or two", say so and it becomes a tolerance band rather than an equality test.

One knock-on: the Fixed Costs header still reads −$136.47 green, because the bucket rolls up on its own spend direction (96.9% of target → good) while its most important item is yellow. Proposal: when any item overrides its direction, the bucket's status becomes worstStatus() of its items — that helper is already exported from shared/csp.js for exactly this.
F5 — the one consequence worth stating out loud, since it is now deliberate. A dollar can appear twice: $100 of groceries charged to the card counts in Groceries, and the $100 you later send the card counts in Debt Payments. That is the direct consequence of counting both, and it is the right call for the reason you gave — but it means bucket percentages are a share of commitments, not of take-home, and can exceed 100% in a heavy-charge month. The identity still holds exactly (bucketTotal + excluded === cashOutflow + cardPurchases) and T37 still asserts it; what changes is the denominator. Proposal: keep the percentages, and when cardPurchases > 0 label the column header % of commitments rather than % of take-home — one honest word rather than a second actual column, which is the double-signal Phase 11 spent four tasks removing.
